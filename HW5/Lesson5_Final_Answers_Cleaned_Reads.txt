Q1. What percentage of reads are properly paired?
Answer: 98.57% (3,102,384 out of 3,147,274 primary reads).
This value was obtained using samtools flagstat on RM11_clean.sam.

Q2. What percentage of reads are properly paired with MAPQ >= 30?
Answer: 85.37% (2,686,798 out of 3,147,274 primary reads).
The numerator was obtained using:
  samtools view -c -f 2 -F 2048 -q 30 RM11_clean.sam
Here, -f 2 requires proper pairing, -F 2048 excludes supplementary alignments,
and -q 30 requires a mapping quality of at least 30.

Q3. How many read pairs map to different chromosomes, before and after filtering?
Before filtering: 4,163 read pairs.
After filtering for proper pairing and MAPQ >= 30: 0 read pairs.
These counts were reported by samtools stats. The reduction is expected
because the proper-pair criterion excludes discordant pairs whose mates
map to different chromosomes in this alignment.

Q4. What is the average sequencing depth?
The mean coverage across ChrIII:77,000-79,000 was 27.33x after filtering.
It was calculated from all 2,001 positions in this region, including
zero-coverage positions, using:
  samtools depth -aa -r ChrIII:77000-79000 RM11_clean_filtered_sorted.bam | awk '{sum+=$3; n++} END {printf "Average depth: %.2fx\n",sum/n}'
Note: 27.33x is the average depth for this selected region, NOT the
average depth across the whole yeast genome.

Q5. What do red and blue read pairs indicate in IGV when colored by insert size?
When alignments are colored by insert size, red indicates a larger-than-
expected insert size, while blue indicates a smaller-than-expected insert
size. These patterns may suggest structural differences, such as deletions
or insertions relative to the reference, but do not prove a variant on
their own.

BONUS Q6. How can sequencing errors be distinguished from real SNPs?
Two sites were examined in IGV using the filtered BAM generated from
the cleaned reads:

(a) Possible sequencing error – ChrIII:77,362
The reference base is G. Of 34 reads covering this position, 33 (97%)
contain G and only one (3%) contains T. The T is supported by just one
forward-strand read and no reverse-strand reads. This isolated mismatch
is more consistent with a sequencing error than with a genuine SNP.

(b) Candidate SNP – ChrIII:77,505
The S288C reference base is A (confirmed with samtools faidx), but all
40 covering reads (100%) contain G: 20 forward-strand and 20 reverse-
strand reads. This consistent A-to-G difference strongly supports a
candidate SNP in RM11 relative to S288C.

Conclusion:
Sequencing errors generally appear as isolated mismatches supported by
very few reads. Genuine SNP candidates show consistent support across
many reads, preferably from both strands. Read quality, mapping quality,
and formal variant calling should also be checked for confirmation.
